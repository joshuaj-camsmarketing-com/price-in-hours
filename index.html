How the estimate is built. Federal tax uses the 2026 IRS brackets and standard deduction for your filing status; pre-tax payroll deductions reduce federal and state taxable income but not payroll tax. FICA is 6.2% Social Security on the first $184,500 of wages plus 1.45% Medicare on all wages, with the extra 0.9% surtax above the threshold for your status. State tax is applied as the flat rate you enter.

If you’re a 1099 contractor. Self-employment tax is 15.3% on 92.35% of net profit — both halves of Social Security and Medicare, because there is no employer paying the other one — with the Social Security half capped at $184,500 and the extra 0.9% Medicare surtax above the threshold for your status. Half of that tax is deducted before income tax, and the 20% qualified business income deduction is applied when claimed. The result is money you owe but nobody collects for you, so the tool treats it as already spent from the day you invoice it.

Two limits worth knowing: the QBI deduction here is modelled below the phase-in threshold, where it is a clean 20%, and is warned about above it, where W-2 wage limits and specified-service rules can cut or eliminate it; and state estimated tax is folded into the same reserve figure at the flat rate you enter. A CPA will beat this on both.

Reading the hours column. Every line in the ledger is priced against your gross hourly rate, so the hours are additive: tax, pre-tax deductions, fixed obligations and what’s left add back up to the hours you actually work. Tax rows are calculated from your earnings, never entered — they are already out of your pay before the fixed obligations are counted, so they are shown separately rather than added to that subtotal.

What “surplus” means here. Take-home pay minus every fixed obligation above, including the savings you treat as non-negotiable, spread across every hour the job costs you — paid or not. That is why the hourly surplus rate is so much lower than your wage, and why a small purchase can cost a startling number of hours.

Itemized deductions, credits, other household income, and local tax are ignored, so treat the take-home figure as a planning estimate rather than a tax return. Everything you enter stays in this browser.
